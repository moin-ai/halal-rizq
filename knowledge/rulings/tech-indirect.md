# Islamic Ruling: Tech Work for Haram Industries (Indirect Involvement)

**Last reviewed:** 2026-06-16
**Consensus level:** Disputed — one of the most commonly asked contemporary questions

---

## The Core Question

A Muslim software engineer applies to build internal tools (HR systems, dashboards, mobile apps) for a company whose primary business is haram (bank, insurer, alcohol brand). Is this permissible?

---

## The Key Principle: Awn ala al-Ithm (Helping in Sin)

> "Help one another in righteousness and piety, but do not help one another in sin and aggression." — Quran 5:2

The scholarly dispute is: does building unrelated IT for a haram institution count as "helping in sin"?

---

## Scholarly Positions

### Position 1: Haram Regardless of Role (Majority / Cautious)

**Who says this:** Ibn Uthaymeen, IslamQA (Sheikh Al-Munajjid), Darul Uloom Deoband, most Hanbali and Hanafi scholars

**Reasoning:**
1. Your salary comes from haram income — you are paid from what Allah prohibited
2. Even indirect contribution strengthens the haram institution (your HR system helps them hire more riba bankers)
3. The hadith on riba explicitly cursed the **writer** and **witness** — those with indirect roles
4. Principle: one who helps establish a haram business carries a share of that sin

**References:**
- IslamQA fatwa 22905 (bank employment)
- IslamQA: https://islamqa.info/en/answers/9987 (working for company that sells haram)
- Darul Uloom Deoband fatwa archive on bank/insurance employment

---

### Position 2: Permissible If Role Is Completely Unrelated (Minority)

**Who says this:** Dr. Yusuf Al-Qaradawi (with strict conditions), some contemporary scholars in necessity contexts

**Reasoning:**
1. The prohibition is on the haram act itself, not on all employment at an institution that also does haram
2. A plumber who fixes the pipes of a bank is not sinful — the plumbing is neutral
3. If the Muslim's work is entirely separated from the haram product, and no haram flows through their hands, it may be permissible

**Conditions (Al-Qaradawi):**
- Zero involvement in the haram product/transaction itself
- No viable halal alternative available (genuine darurah/necessity)
- Actively seeking halal alternative
- This is a temporary state, not a comfortable long-term career choice

**Why most scholars reject this:**
- Salary still comes from haram earnings
- Sustaining the institution sustains the haram
- The plumber analogy breaks down: a permanent employee contributes to the institution's growth and capacity, unlike a one-time contractor

---

### Position 3: Distinguish by Degree of Connection (Some Contemporary Scholars)

A more nuanced scale used by some scholars:

| Type of Work | Ruling |
|---|---|
| Directly building the haram product | ❌ Haram — no dispute |
| Building a tool used exclusively by haram department | ❌ Haram |
| Building shared infrastructure that haram department also uses | 🟠 Mukhtalaf |
| Building internal tool with no connection to haram product | 🟡 Mashbooh (majority say still haram; minority permit) |

## Practical Verdict for Evaluation

Given **profile.yml setting: mashbooh_tolerance = strict**, apply the majority position:

| Scenario | Applied Ruling |
|----------|----------------|
| Building a data dashboard for a riba bank | ❌ Haram |
| Building internal HR software for a conventional insurer | ❌ Haram (majority) |
| Building a mobile app for an alcohol brand | ❌ Haram |
| Building internal IT for a halal company that has one haram client | 🟡 Mashbooh — evaluate client exposure |
| Building IT for a company where haram is <5% of revenue (e.g., hotel that serves alcohol) | 🟠 Mukhtalaf — present all opinions |
| Building IT for a company with no haram in core business but poor ESG (e.g., environmentally harmful) | ✅ Generally Halal — not an Islamic prohibition |

---

## Recommended Approach in Evaluations

1. Identify the company's primary revenue source
2. If haram: apply ❌ Haram (majority) with note of minority opinion
3. If mixed: calculate haram revenue proportion and note scholarly positions
4. Always flag: "even if your role is unrelated, the majority position is haram because salary derives from haram income"
5. If the candidate has no halal alternative (genuine darurah): note the minority position and its strict conditions
