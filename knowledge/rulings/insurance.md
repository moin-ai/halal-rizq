# Islamic Ruling: Conventional Insurance

**Last reviewed:** 2026-06-16
**Consensus level:** Strong majority — haram, with takaful as the halal alternative

---

## Why Conventional Insurance Is Haram

Conventional insurance contains three elements that Islamic jurisprudence prohibits:

1. **Gharar (excessive uncertainty):** The insured pays premiums without knowing if/when they will receive a payout. The outcome is fundamentally uncertain for both parties.
2. **Maysir (gambling):** The contract resembles gambling — you may pay in full and receive nothing, or receive far more than you paid.
3. **Riba (interest):** Insurance companies invest premiums in interest-bearing instruments, and the profit model includes riba.

**Quranic basis:**
> "O you who believe! Do not consume one another's wealth unjustly, but only through trade by mutual consent." — Quran 4:29

**Scholarly consensus:** The Islamic Fiqh Academy (OIC) Resolution 9/2 (1985) ruled conventional insurance haram and declared takaful (mutual cooperative insurance) the permissible alternative.

---

## Scholarly Positions

### Position 1: Haram — Strong Majority

**Who says this:** Islamic Fiqh Academy (OIC), AAOIFI, Ibn Uthaymeen, Ibn Baz, Darul Uloom Deoband, IslamQA, virtually all contemporary scholars

**Ruling:** Conventional insurance is haram. Working for a conventional insurance company sustains a haram institution and is therefore haram.

**References:**
- Islamic Fiqh Academy Resolution 9/2 (Second Session, 1985)
- IslamQA fatwa 8889: https://islamqa.info/en/answers/8889
- Ibn Uthaymeen: Fatawa Al-Mar'ah, on insurance

---

### Position 2: Some Health/Life Insurance Permitted Out of Necessity (Very Minority)

**Who says this:** Some scholars in Western contexts where takaful is unavailable (darurah position)

**Ruling:** If no takaful alternative exists and the law requires insurance (e.g., mandatory car insurance), one may obtain the minimum required under necessity. This does NOT extend to *working* at an insurance company.

## Distinction: InsurTech / Embedded Insurance

Many tech companies now embed insurance products. Evaluate:
- Is the company's primary product insurance? → Follow full insurance ruling
- Is insurance an incidental feature of a larger halal product? → Mashbooh; evaluate the role
- Would you be building the insurance component? → Haram if so; evaluate if purely other components
