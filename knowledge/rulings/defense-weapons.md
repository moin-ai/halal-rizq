# Islamic Ruling: Defense, Weapons & Military Contractors

**Last reviewed:** 2026-06-16
**Consensus level:** Context-dependent — supplying oppressors is clearly haram; defensive weapons for Muslims is permissible

---

## Core Principle

Islam does not prohibit weapons or defense in principle. The Prophet ﷺ wore armor, and Muslims are commanded to prepare strength:

> "Prepare against them whatever you are able of power and of steeds of war by which you may terrify the enemy of Allah and your enemy." — Quran 8:60

The prohibition is on **oppression (zulm)** and supplying those who commit it.

> "Help one another in righteousness and piety, but do not help one another in sin and aggression." — Quran 5:2

---

## Scholarly Framework

### What is clearly Haram

1. **Manufacturing or selling weapons to oppressive regimes** that use them against civilians, Muslims, or any innocent people — ijma' (consensus)
2. **Building surveillance or targeting software used against innocent people** — extends the same prohibition to tech
3. **Working for companies whose primary client base is oppressive military forces** — facilitating the oppression
4. **Building autonomous weapons (AI/drone targeting) for armies committing atrocities** — unambiguously haram

### What is clearly Halal

1. **Working in defense for a Muslim country defending its land** — permissible
2. **Building defensive systems (shields, medical tech, communication for civilian protection)** — generally permissible
3. **General IT for a military that is not engaged in oppression** — scholarly disagreement, lean mashbooh

### Scholarly Positions on Working at Defense Contractors

**Position 1 — Haram if the company sells to oppressors (Majority)**

If the defense company's primary customers include governments actively killing civilians/Muslims (Israel, Myanmar military, Indian forces in Kashmir, etc.):
- Working there, regardless of your role, is haram
- Your salary funds the oppression
- You are part of the supply chain of zulm

**Scholars:** Ibn Uthaymeen, Ibn Baz, IslamQA; extends from Quran 5:2 principle

**Position 2 — Evaluate the specific work (Minority)**

Some scholars argue: if a defense company sells to both good and bad actors, and your specific work is on a neutral product (e.g., internal HR software, not a weapons guidance system), it may be permissible with strong dislike (karahah).

**Critique:** Rejected by most scholars because your salary and contribution still funds the company's capacity to produce oppressive weapons.
